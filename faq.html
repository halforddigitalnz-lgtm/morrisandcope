The difference is where it sits. We treat accurate books as the groundwork for good advice rather than the end product, so if you would like the advisory side later it is already there to build on.

A bookkeeper records daily financial transactions — sales, purchases and payments. They can help with invoicing clients and managing your accounts payable, process payroll and file PAYE, and prepare and file GST returns.

A business advisor uses those records to help you decide what to do next: where cashflow is heading, which numbers to watch, what a job or product is really costing you, and where a process is losing you time or money.

An accountant uses the same data to analyse annual performance, prepare annual financial statements, and file annual income tax returns. In New Zealand that work almost always requires a qualified accountant, and we do not do it. We keep the records they need in the format they want, which usually makes their job faster and your bill smaller.
